# Proposal: The Support Portfolio

**Subject:** The Support Portfolio — emotional support and social connection are distributed across a partner, friends, family, and community. The number of available channels, how concentrated support becomes in one relationship, and who performs the work of maintaining those channels are separate variables. “Mankeeping” is an emerging gendered instance, not the name of the whole mechanism.

**Suggested parent entry:** a sub-entry or extension under `frameworks:third-party-layer`, whose current approval/veto model names what networks do *to* a couple but not what support networks do *for* the partners. Cross-link `frameworks:ownership-load` for the anticipating/arranging/monitoring work and the existing relationships-through-history passage about loading many institutional functions onto one partner.

## Proposed claims and evidence tiers

1. **Friendship and romantic support are linked, but they are neither simple substitutes nor one uniformly beneficial bundle.** In five annual waves of an Australian cohort, 526 partnered men ages 28–32 reported peer investment and relationship quality. Very little friend time and very small close networks were associated with poorer romantic quality; the association improved up to a high “tipping point,” with weaker evidence of decline beyond it. Separately, a U.S. adult sample (N = 940) found that people naming a romantic partner as their best friend reported more companionship, while those with a separate best friend reported more social support; romantic relationship quality did not differ between respondents labeling their partner a best friend versus a friend. **Suggested tier: Tier 2** observational association, with cross-sectional designs, role-label selection, and wide uncertainty at the Australian study's high-investment tail.  
   Primary sources: [Marabel-Whitburn et al., “Balancing friends and romance” (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1177/02654075231198441) and [Pennington et al., “What's in a label?” (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1177/02654075251336872).

2. **Gender differences in outside support do not establish that men's partners are usually their sole support channel.** A probability-panel report found women more likely than men to have received emotional support from a friend in the prior week (41% versus 21%), while a later Pew probability-panel survey found men and women equally likely to say they would turn to a spouse or partner for emotional support. The measured asymmetry is in some non-romantic channels, not proof of universal partner exclusivity. **Suggested tier: Tier 2** descriptive population evidence and a hard boundary on the popular claim.  
   Primary sources: [Survey Center on American Life, *The State of American Friendship* (2021)](https://www.americansurveycenter.org/research/the-state-of-american-friendship-change-challenges-and-loss/) and [Pew Research Center, *Where men and women turn for emotional support and social connection* (2025)](https://www.pewresearch.org/social-trends/2025/01/16/where-men-and-women-turn-for-emotional-support-and-social-connection/).

3. **Social-network support work can be measured separately from ordinary partner care, but its downstream effects remain provisional.** A new Mankeeping Scale–Partner Report recovered six dimensions—including social-event curation, perceived partner dependence, support inequality, and impact on the woman's time/wellbeing—in 402 U.S. cisgender women partnered with men; the factor structure was replicated in 395 comparable U.K. participants. In the U.S. sample, higher scores correlated with lower relationship satisfaction and higher distress, burden, and burnout. **Suggested tier: Tier 2 for the measurement structure; Tier 3 for consequence claims**, because the validation and outcomes are cross-sectional, same-reporter, and partly share burden content.  
   Primary source: [Mancini et al., “Measuring Mankeeping” (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11199-026-01661-2).

4. **The proposed gendered mechanism is narrower than generic emotional labor.** Mankeeping refers specifically to unreciprocated work that compensates for deficits in a man's other social ties—such as arranging social contact or becoming the concentrated emotional channel—not every act of listening, scheduling, or care a woman performs. Its founding paper presented three testable postulates rather than prevalence or causal estimates. **Suggested tier: Lens** until longitudinal, dyadic, and comparative tests exist.  
   Primary theoretical source: [Ferrara & Vergara, “Theorizing mankeeping” (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1037/men0000494).

## Candidate common misreadings

- A man's partner naturally becomes his whole support system once the relationship turns serious.
- Women lose interest in dating because every man expects one partner to manage his friendships.
- More friends always improve a man's relationship because outside support removes pressure from his partner.

## Boundary conditions

- Network size, support quality, support concentration, reciprocity, and the labor of arranging contact are different measurements. A person can have many contacts and one confidant, or few contacts with distributed support.
- Emotional support between partners is an ordinary relationship good. The proposed burden mechanism requires concentration and/or unreciprocated coordination work; receiving care alone does not satisfy it.
- The men's peer-network study is observational, limited to Australian men entering their thirties, and estimates cross-sectional associations across repeated waves. Romantic quality may influence friendship investment as readily as the reverse.
- The partner-as-best-friend study is cross-sectional and compares self-selected labels, companionship, and perceived support. It does not show that merging roles causes support to shrink or companionship to increase.
- The Mankeeping Scale currently rests on cisgender women in heterosexual relationships longer than six months in the U.S. and U.K. It has no partner reports, behavioral validation, prevalence estimate, longitudinal ordering, or comparison showing unique prediction beyond established emotional/cognitive labor constructs.
- The new scale validates a way to measure the proposed construct. It does not establish that the construct is common, that women generally perform it, or that it causes distress or dissatisfaction.
- The friendship surveys establish sex differences in selected channels under specific question wording and recall windows. They do not measure a “sole channel” or show that women repair men's networks.
- Friendship and romance provide overlapping but non-interchangeable forms of connection; expanding one channel is no guarantee of satisfying needs in another.
- Objective isolation, relationship count, support distribution, and subjective loneliness are distinct. Being partnered or having multiple channels does not guarantee feeling heard, understood, or connected.

## Deliberately not claimed

- Men generally have no friends or use a romantic partner as their only confidant.
- Women are naturally better social coordinators, or men are biologically unable to maintain supportive friendships.
- Mankeeping explains the sex gap in dating interest, women's celibacy, or a broad retreat from relationships.
- Every act of emotional support, calendar planning, or introducing a partner to friends is burdensome labor.
- Adding friends causes a man's romantic relationship to improve, or very large networks are uniformly beneficial.
- A woman partner causes a man's social isolation, or bears sole responsibility for repairing it.
- Friends can replace romantic intimacy for everyone, or romance can replace friendship without tradeoffs.
