# PROPOSAL P1 - The Verification Stack

**Subject:** Verification is a staged, scoped process for testing claims as romantic trust and stakes rise. No badge, image search, live call, platform, outsider opinion, or meeting certifies the whole person. Because adversaries can imitate safety workflows, the provenance of a verification event is itself part of the test.

**Parent entry:** `frameworks:signal-cost-rule` (**The Signal Cost Rule**). The parent asks what a claim would cost to fake. This child specifies how several partially independent checks can raise that cost across identity, embodiment, story continuity, money requests, and time. `frameworks:third-party-layer` is related because trusted outsiders can supply independent judgment.

## Sourced tiered claims

1. **Tier 2 - primary process and detection evidence:** Whitty (2013) interviewed 20 financial and non-financial victims; the institutional abstract reports grooming before financial requests, later stages that sustain involvement, and near-win reasoning as a proposed explanation for continuation and re-victimization. Whitty (2019) asked 261 participants to classify scam and genuine profiles; its abstract reports several correlates of accuracy, says detection was difficult, and proposes an explicit human-detection stage. Together they support staged, fallible detection rather than prevalence, subgroup risk, or a validated practice checklist. DOI [`10.1093/bjc/azt009`](https://doi.org/10.1093/bjc/azt009); DOI [`10.1108/JFC-06-2018-0053`](https://doi.org/10.1108/JFC-06-2018-0053).
2. **Tier 3 - official observed threat pattern:** The FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center warns that fraudsters use fake dating-platform verification links framed as protection, supported by fabricated media legitimacy, to obtain identity and payment information and recurring charges. This establishes adversarial verification theater, not its frequency. [IC3 advisory, 26 April 2024](https://www.ic3.gov/PSA/2024/PSA240426).
3. **Tier 3 - official practice guidance:** The FTC describes off-platform movement, frequent trust-building contact, adaptive stories, inability to meet, and hard-to-reverse payment requests. Its practices span different evidence channels: consult a trusted person, compare story patterns, reverse-search an image, keep a pre-meeting money boundary, and report through payment and platform channels. Guidance supports a layered practice model; it supplies no sensitivity, specificity, or guarantee. [FTC, "What To Know About Romance Scams"](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-know-about-romance-scams).
4. **Tier 1 - randomized primary gray literature:** Ofcom reports an online randomized controlled trial with 4,201 UK adults comparing a serious game, text guidance, and an unrelated-topic control. The game improved scam-message detection relative to both comparators, the difference remained significant after four weeks, and the study found no backfire toward legitimate messages. This supports training detection rather than certifying a particular partner. [Ofcom research page and technical report, 16 July 2025](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/online-fraud/harnessing-the-power-of-serious-games-to-protect-people-against-romance-fraud).

## Common misreadings

- A verified profile proves a partner's identity, intent, financial story, and future conduct.
- A successful video call makes sending money to a distant romantic contact safe.
- Intimate disclosure from a partner should accelerate confidence in every later request.

## Boundaries

- Every check has a claim scope. A platform badge may attest to a platform procedure; it does not attest to intention, financial need, future conduct, or compatibility.
- A live call supplies evidence that someone controlled a stream at a moment. It does not by itself establish legal identity, life history, intent, or financial safety.
- A reverse-image hit that shows reuse or inconsistent identity is informative. An empty search is inconclusive because indexing and source coverage vary.
- Story consistency across time and channels can raise or lower confidence. A coherent story remains reproducible by a prepared adversary.
- Staying on-platform preserves platform controls and reporting context. It does not create immunity, and legitimate privacy, disability, abuse, or work constraints can shape communication choices.
- Consulting trusted outsiders adds an independent evidence channel. Their concern is information, not transferred decision authority.
- Refusing, delaying, or modifying a requested check can have legitimate explanations. The refusal is evidence to interpret alongside stakes, alternatives, and the surrounding pattern.
- Meeting publicly changes the evidence channel and introduces physical-safety practice. It does not certify subsequent behavior.
- As requested trust, money, data access, travel, or secrecy rises, the needed independence and cost of verification should rise too.

## Deliberate nonclaims

- No demographic group is declared the default victim; M-TBD-65 remains the separate population-and-loss ruling.
- No individual check is assigned a detection rate, false-positive rate, or universal rank.
- No universal timetable requires documents, calls, location disclosure, or an in-person meeting.
- No failed check transfers responsibility from the deceiver to the target.
- No platform verification feature is declared useless; each feature is judged by its actual procedure and claim scope.
- No safety check predicts compatibility or guarantees freedom from coercion, abuse, or later deception.

## Proposed standard

Treat verification as a stack, not a seal. Name the claim being tested, the channel supplying evidence, who controls that channel, what a false claim would cost, and what remains untested. Increase independence across checks as requested stakes rise. A discrepancy can be diagnostic; a clean result only updates the claim within that check's scope.
