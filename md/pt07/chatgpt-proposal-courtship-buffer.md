# PROPOSAL P3 - The Courtship Buffer

**Subject:** Alcohol can change the emotional conditions around early social contact: perceived threat, positive affect, bonding, and the movement from attraction toward possible interaction. A sober meeting removes that state-dependent buffer; it does not automatically reveal a truer self or superior compatibility. Name the rung and state actually observed before inferring courtship consequences.

**Parent entry:** `frameworks:conversion-ladder` (**The Conversion Ladder**). The parent separates attention, desire, choice, and durability. This child names a temporary state modifier that can change movement between early rungs without proving movement to mutual interest, commitment, or durability. `frameworks:interaction-gate` is related because live interaction supplies richer evidence, while the third-spaces deep dive remains the owner of venue availability.

## Sourced tiered claims

1. **Tier 1 - randomized primary experiment:** Sayette et al. assigned 720 social drinkers to three-stranger groups receiving alcohol, placebo, or no-alcohol control beverages. Alcohol increased observed positive affect and group coordination, reduced observed negative affect, and elevated self-reported bonding. The design supports an acute social-bonding mechanism in initial group formation; triad bonding is not romantic chemistry. DOI [`10.1177/0956797611435134`](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797611435134).
2. **Tier 1 - counterbalanced primary experiment:** Bowdring and Sayette tested 36 male social drinkers in alcohol and control sessions. Conventional attractiveness ratings did not change, while participants selected more of the most attractive targets for a hypothetical future interaction after alcohol. The selection task was not preregistered, involved no actual approach, lacked a placebo, and ended early because of COVID. DOI [`10.15288/jsad.22-00355`](https://doi.org/10.15288/jsad.22-00355).
3. **Tier 1 - quantitative synthesis:** Bowdring and Sayette's meta-analysis found a small average association between alcohol consumption and enhanced ratings of others' physical attractiveness across the available literature. The later experiment's null rating result and the meta-analysis's small magnitude separate a possible perception effect from the more specific initiation effect. DOI [`10.1111/add.14227`](https://doi.org/10.1111/add.14227).
4. **Tier 2 - longitudinal observational evidence, with Tier 3 practice language:** Hamilton, Armeli, and Tennen analyzed 43,837 daily reports from 540 mostly undergraduate participants. Receiving a drink offer on a given night was associated with greater odds of reporting sex while controlling for alcohol consumed; accepted-drink count was unrelated. Because the study did not identify the offerer, sexual partner, exact timing, or causality, it supports an alcohol-coded social-cue pathway rather than a dose effect or relationship formation. The TIME sober-dating essay separately describes alcohol as a familiar buffer around asking someone out, rejection concern, self-disclosure, sexual pacing, and opening up; those observations supply practice questions, not comparative causal evidence. DOI [`10.1080/07448481.2022.2086435`](https://doi.org/10.1080/07448481.2022.2086435); [TIME, "The Perks of Sober Dating"](https://time.com/7205261/consider-sober-dating-essay/).

## Common misreadings

- Alcohol reveals courtship chemistry by showing how easily two people bond and approach each other.
- A sober encounter exposes a partner's authentic personality and lasting character.
- Liquid courage makes courtship conversation easier and a person's intentions seem clearer.

## Boundaries

- The large experiment measured three-person stranger-group bonding, not pair-specific attraction, romantic interest, consent, or relationship formation.
- The selection experiment measured choices among images for a hypothetical later interaction. It did not observe conversation initiation, reciprocation, total approach volume, or successful formation.
- A state-dependent positive social experience can be genuine in that state. It is neither a compatibility certificate nor necessarily a false feeling.
- A sober interaction can feel quieter, slower, or more effortful because a familiar buffer is absent. That difference does not identify a fixed authentic self.
- Alcohol dose, expectancy, drinking history, setting, and interaction partners can change the response. The two experiments cover young adult social drinkers under controlled doses.
- An alcohol-coded cue such as a drink offer may mark opportunity, interest, reciprocity pressure, or a drinking context. It does not reveal offerer intent, consent, or a pharmacological effect.
- The group experiment's placebo and control pattern supports a pharmacological contribution for its measured outcomes. The selection experiment lacked a placebo and cannot separate pharmacology from expectancy.
- The attractiveness meta-analysis addresses ratings, while the selection study addresses a later decision. These are different Conversion Ladder rungs.
- The scout evidence does not test alcohol's effects on disclosure quality, trust accuracy, sexual pacing, consent communication, compatibility judgment, or relationship durability.
- Sobriety or drinking preference can itself be a substantive compatibility value. That identity/value screen is separate from the acute interaction mechanism.
- Venue loss, bar availability, and the supply of alcohol-free third spaces remain outside this entry.
- Social reward and risk can coexist. Naming a buffer is not a recommendation to use it.

## Deliberate nonclaims

- No recommendation is made to drink, abstain, conceal sobriety, or disclose it on a universal schedule.
- No causal effect is assigned to relationship formation, mutual desire, satisfaction, commitment, or durability.
- No sober meeting is declared universally safer, more authentic, less intimate, or more compatible.
- No group-bonding measure or drink offer is treated as romantic chemistry, sexual interest, or consent.
- No bar, restaurant, app, or third-space trend is attributed to sobriety.
- No Dry January or alcohol-free menu statistic is proposed.
- No practitioner claim about disclosure, sexual intensity, trust speed, or opening up is promoted to evidence.
- No result is generalized to recovery populations, abstainers, people with alcohol-use disorder, older adults, or every dosage pattern.

## Proposed standard

When alcohol appears in a formation claim, identify the Conversion Ladder rung and the measured state: attractiveness rating, felt threat, positive affect, bonding, attempted interaction, reciprocation, or later outcome. Treat evidence at that rung as local. Compare sober and alcohol-involved interaction without converting either state into proof of chemistry, character, consent, compatibility, or durability.
